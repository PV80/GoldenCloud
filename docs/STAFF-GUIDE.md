# Your GoldenCloud drive

Welcome. This gives you a new drive on your PC — **`G:`** — that holds your work
files on the company's own storage in the office. You can reach it from
anywhere: at your desk, at home, on a train.

It behaves exactly like a USB stick or a normal folder. Drag things in, drag
things out, open them, save them. There is nothing new to learn.

**Only you can see what is in your folder.** Not your colleagues, not anyone
outside the company. Everyone has their own.

---

## What you have been given

Three things, probably in two separate messages:

1. **`GoldenCloudSetup.exe`** — the installer.
2. **Your username** — usually your first name, all lower case.
3. **Your password** — sent separately, or told to you in person.

If you are missing any of these, ask before going further.

---

## Setting it up (about five minutes, once)

**1.** Save `GoldenCloudSetup.exe` somewhere you can find it, like your Desktop,
and double-click it.

**2.** Windows will probably show a blue box saying **"Windows protected your
PC"**. This is expected and it is not a virus warning — it appears for any
program Microsoft has not seen many times before.

Click **More info**, then click **Run anyway**.

**3.** Click through the installer, saying yes to everything. Partway through,
Windows will ask *"Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your
device?"* — click **Yes**. It is installing the part that makes the drive
appear.

**4.** When it finishes, a small GoldenCloud icon appears at the bottom right of
your screen, near the clock. You may need to click the little **^** arrow to see
it.

**5.** Click that icon. A small window asks for your username and password.

There is **no server address to type** — it is already built in. If you are
being asked for a web address, something is wrong; ask for help.

**6.** Type your username and password and click Sign in.

> Your password will not show as you type — that is normal. Type carefully.
> Capital letters matter: `Alice` is not the same as `alice`.

**7.** After a few seconds you will see a message saying your drive is ready.

That is it. You never have to do this again.

---

## Where your drive is

Open **File Explorer** — the yellow folder icon on your taskbar, or press the
**Windows key + E**.

Click **This PC** on the left. Your drive is there, called **`G:`**.

Double-click it to open. It will be empty to start with. Drag your files in.

**Tip:** right-click `G:` and choose **Pin to Quick access** so it is always at
the top of the left-hand list.

## Using it day to day

- **Save things into it** the way you save anything else — in Word, Excel, or
  anything, choose *Save As* and pick `G:`.
- **Your files are not on your PC.** They are in the office. That is the point:
  if your laptop is lost, stolen, or dies, your work is untouched.
- **You can work from anywhere** with an internet connection. Home, a hotel, a
  phone hotspot. It all works the same.
- **It reconnects by itself.** Turn your PC on, log in, and `G:` is there. You
  will not be asked for your password again.
- **You only see your own folder.** There is no way to see a colleague's files
  and no way for them to see yours.

---

## Three things worth knowing

**Deleting is permanent.** Files deleted from `G:` do **not** go to the Recycle
Bin. If you delete something by mistake, tell whoever runs the system
**straight away** — the sooner you say, the better the chance of getting it back
from a backup.

**Big files take as long as your internet takes.** Copying a 2 GB video will not
be instant when you are working from home. Start it and let it run; do not
cancel and retry, which just starts it over.

**Lots of small files are slow.** Copying 5,000 small files takes much longer
than one big file of the same total size. If you are moving a whole project
folder, right-click it → **Send to** → **Compressed (zipped) folder** first,
copy the single zip, and unzip it at the other end.

## If your drive disappears

`G:` vanishing is almost always one of four things. Work down the list.

**1. Are you online?** Open a web page. If nothing loads, the drive cannot work
either. Fix your internet and the drive comes back on its own.

**2. Is the GoldenCloud icon still there,** at the bottom right near the clock?
Click the **^** arrow to see hidden icons.

- **If the icon is missing:** the app is not running. Click **Start**, type
  `GoldenCloud`, and press Enter to start it again.
- **If the icon is there:** click it. It will tell you what it thinks the
  problem is, and usually offer a **Reconnect** button. Click it.

**3. Restart your PC.** Genuinely — a proper restart fixes this more often than
anything else, because it clears out a stuck connection. Click **Start** → power
icon → **Restart** (not *Shut down*, which on Windows is not the same thing).

**4. Sign out and back in.** Click the GoldenCloud icon → **Sign out**, then
click it again and sign in with your username and password.

If none of that works, ask for help — and say **which of these four you already
tried**. It saves a lot of time.

## If you forget your password

There is no self-service reset. Ask whoever set this up for you; they can set a
new one in about fifteen seconds.

When they do, your drive will stop working until you sign out and sign back in
with the new password. That is deliberate — it is what makes changing a password
actually protect you.

---

## If your laptop is lost or stolen

**Tell whoever runs the system immediately.** Not tomorrow — now, even if it is
the weekend.

They can lock your account out in seconds, which stops anyone getting to your
files from that machine. Your files themselves are safe: they are in the office,
not on the laptop.
